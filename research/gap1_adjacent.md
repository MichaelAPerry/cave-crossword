## IMPORTANT: prior art that overlaps the core gap
Anbulagan & Botea, "Crossword Puzzles as a Constraint Problem", CP 2008, LNCS 5202 pp. 550-554, DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-85958-1_40 (abstract at https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/crossword-puzzles-as-a-constraint-problem/). Abstract: phase transition occurs when varying dictionary size and number of blocked cells on large realistic grids; hard classes identified. A 2021 AAAI paper (unverified, via search snippet) cites a 2009 Botea/Anbulagan analysis of "easy-hard-easy" behaviour. So "phase transition vs dictionary size / density" is PARTIAL-DONE for generic dictionaries. What appears NOT done (could not find): real vs synthetic matched lexicon, sharpness/finite-size scaling. Note it is a 5-page short paper; details not read.

## Ranking of top 3 OPEN/PARTIAL adjacent questions (novelty x feasibility x value)
1. Item 3: lexicon statistics that predict fillability / threshold location (substitution degree, positional entropy, bigram tightness), tested across a family of synthetic lexicons. Novel (no linking work found), cheap, and turns the core comparison into a mechanism.
2. Item 1: solution-space structure (log-count, backbone, clustering) of small grids vs lexicon size, real vs synthetic. Sudoku template exists (Prates & Lamb), crosswords untouched; feasible at 5x5/6x6.
3. Item 7 (or 4): learned fill-feasibility predictor for partial grids using item-3 features; fall-back is item 4, replicating Long's "support" curves to quantify the real-vs-random 1.5x factor. Item 2 (cross-language) is most novel but ranks lower on feasibility/confounds.

## Undercutting items
- Anbulagan & Botea 2008 already reports crossword phase transitions vs dictionary size and blocked cells (generic dictionaries). The core gap survives only in its "real vs matched synthetic lexicon, sharpness" part.
- Long 1993 (Word Ways) already compared real word lists to a probabilistic model for word squares (real needs ~1.5x support): a partial prior on "real English shifts the threshold".
