# WordLife prior art and research critique

**Web access was available and used.** I checked publication records, author project pages, repositories, and upstream license texts where retrievable. The search was exploratory. I treat project descriptions as evidence of documented mechanics and flag cases where I could not verify implementation details or redistribution terms.

## 1. Prior art and what to borrow

### Language on a grid

- **Joel Swanson, *Textual Automata* (2019, Electronic Literature Organization conference, University College Cork).** The artist describes a Conway-inspired work where a user supplies a word and growth or death depends on whether a synonym is found within a limited number of attempts. This is the closest conceptual precedent I found. An actual linguistic relation governs the dynamics. The abstract establishes the synonym mechanism and the artistic objective, with little detail on spatial coupling, heredity, or measurement. Borrow the distinction between text used as a display and lexical relations that actually change which transitions occur. Treat the synonym-lookup mechanism as a useful comparator for spelling-based growth. [ELO2019 program, printed p. 147](https://cora.ucc.ie/bitstreams/e2222636-c8ae-4220-ab0b-80c6e78b3807/download#page=150)
- **Greg Kreisman, English-word cellular automata (artist portfolio, undated), and Stephen Wolfram, *Cellular Automaton Fonts* (2007, Wolfram Demonstrations).** Kreisman documents a word/CA artwork in his portfolio. Its linked demo was inaccessible in this review, so the role of English words in the update rule is **uncertain**. Wolfram documents a separate technique in which the bitmap of a letter seeds a 2D CA and resulting shapes often remain recognizable. Together these establish relevant visual precedents. Borrow the idea of testing whether the same motion persists under relabeling, which distinguishes the visual contribution of letters from their causal contribution. [Kreisman portfolio](https://greggelong.github.io/) · [Wolfram Demonstration](https://demonstrations.wolfram.com/CellularAutomatonFonts/)
- **Lawrence J. Mazlack (1976, *SIAM Journal on Computing*) and Matthew L. Ginsberg (2011, *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research*).** Mazlack documented computer generation of crosswords. Ginsberg's *Dr.Fill* solves clue-bearing American crosswords as singly weighted constraint satisfaction problems. Entries have candidate domains and crossing positions must agree, while candidate weights express clue plausibility. This already establishes much of the machinery for valid intersecting words on a fixed slot layout. Borrow candidate filtering and support counts at crossing letters, and use a constructor as a baseline for static density and length distributions. A solver's search and backtracking trace can look animated even when every step serves a single finished board. [Mazlack](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0205004) · [Ginsberg](https://m.aaai.org/Papers/JAIR/Vol42/JAIR4222.pdf)
- **Paul Merrell (2007, i3D), Maxim Gumin (2016, GitHub), and Isaac Karth and Adam M. Smith (2017, FDG).** Example-based model synthesis and Wave Function Collapse (WFC) build larger configurations using local compatibility. Karth and Smith explain WFC as constraint solving. Local relations can trigger propagation across the board, and common implementations choose a location by inspecting uncertainty across many cells. Borrow overlap compatibility, contradiction counts, and a comparison against a greedy local update. Keep the radius of an individual relation distinct from the observation radius and schedule of the solver. [Merrell's paper and project](https://paulmerrell.org/model-synthesis/) · [Gumin's WFC repository](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse) · [Karth and Smith](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1f29235t)
- **Garrett Newman / gasnew, *Crosswyrd* (2022, public project).** An explicit precedent for WFC-based dense crossword construction. Its documented uses include autofill, suggestions, and a constraint heatmap. Borrow the heatmap as a way to visualize how much freedom a crossing leaves and use filled grids to test whether a proposed statistic can distinguish a dynamic ecosystem from a tight packing of words. [Crosswyrd repository and linked original announcement](https://github.com/gasnew/crosswyrd)
- **Lewis Carroll, *Doublets* (1879, Macmillan).** Changing one letter at a time through legal words gives a same-length substitution graph. This is already a model of viable point mutation. It has no spatial constraint until embedded in the grid, where the same edit can affect two words at once. Borrow connected components, position-specific viable degree, and distance to a viable variant. Add insertion, deletion, and attested morphology as separately labeled edges so their contributions can be measured. [Carroll's text](https://schnark.github.io/lewis-carroll/html/texts/doublets-a-word-puzzle.html)
- **Commercial and recreational games.** Hasbro's *Scrabble* rules and Newtoy's *Words With Friends* (2009, now Zynga) establish player-driven competition for board space using interlocking words. Borrow their scarcity of useful crossing letters and the distinction between existing tiles and new placements. *Bananagrams* documents simultaneous play on private, rearrangeable grids fed by a shared tile pool. Borrow the difference between competition for a common resource and direct interference at a crossing. David Parlett's documented *Wordsworth* variant scores words along both axes of a grid as letters are called. Borrow its simple axis-based census as a packing comparison. [Hasbro rules](https://instructions.hasbro.com/en-us/instruction/scrabble-board-game) · [Zynga franchise history](https://www.zynga.com/about/our-story/) · [Bananagrams instructions](https://bananagrams.com/pages/instructions) · [Parlett's Wordsworth rules](https://www.parlettgames.uk/wgames/wdsworth.html)
- **Games with words that change the board.** Zach Gage's *SpellTower* (2011, iOS) ties word selection and tile removal to a changing letter field. Borrow the idea that lexical success can clear space and change future opportunities. Brainseed Factory's *Typoman* forms words from letters to change the puzzle environment, and Arvi Teikari's *Baba Is You* (2019) lets players rearrange word tokens that express executable rules. These are prior art for words with effects. Borrow their clear feedback between a linguistic operation and a physical consequence. Their curated effects or grammar would be an explicit design intervention in a WordLife experiment. [Gage's project](https://stfj.net/art/2012/SpellTower/) · [Typoman](https://www.typoman.net/) · [Baba Is You](https://www.hempuli.com/baba/)

I searched the indexed web and used GitHub-targeted queries for “word game of life,” “lexical cellular automaton,” “crossword automaton,” and “word evolution simulation,” plus combinations involving word ecology, letter growth, WFC, and dictionary mutation. I found the projects above through those searches and adjacent queries. I did not verify an implementation combining natural-language word validity, shared-letter interactions, and sustained heritable turnover. **“Letter Life” is uncertain** as a direct antecedent because I found no sufficiently documented matching project under that name. GitHub web indexing and surviving project descriptions leave gaps, particularly for demos and student projects.

### Artificial life, ecology, and language evolution

- **Walter Fontana (1992, *Artificial Life II*), Fontana and Leo W. Buss (1994, *Bulletin of Mathematical Biology*), and Peter Dittrich, Jens Ziegler, and Wolfgang Banzhaf (2001, *Artificial Life*).** AlChemy makes symbolic expressions both participants and products of reactions and studies self-maintaining organizations. The review describes how to specify an artificial chemistry by its objects, reaction rules, and reaction dynamics. This is a close functional analogy if words or morphemes transform each other. Borrow the distinction between a string that merely persists and an organization whose components regenerate each other. Identify where the scheduler and resource supply enter the model. [Fontana](https://sites.santafe.edu/~walter/Papers/alchemy92.pdf) · [Fontana and Buss](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/walterfontana/files/arrival.pdf) · [Dittrich et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11712956/)
- **Tim J. Hutton (2002 and 2007, *Artificial Life*).** Squirm3 implements evolvable symbolic replicators in an artificial chemistry. Hutton's later 2D work demonstrates reproducing compartments whose membranes keep useful enzymes near their producers. The connection to WordLife is especially strong where spatial interactions determine who benefits from symbolic products. Borrow the question of whether a useful crossing preferentially benefits the lineage that produced it, or is available to any opportunist nearby. [Squirm3 paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12650644/) · [2D compartments paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17204010/)
- **Thomas S. Ray (1991, *Artificial Life II*) and Charles Ofria and Claus O. Wilke (2004, *Artificial Life*).** Tierra evolved replicating instruction sequences under competition for computational resources and produced parasites. Avida made digital evolution experimentally tractable with heritable programs, mutations, and configurable environments. Both have a clearer account of descent than a board census of repeated strings. Borrow genealogy logs and competition for an explicit limiting resource. Track which local events transmit a pattern and which produce the same spelling independently. [Ray](https://tomray.me/pubs/alife2/Ray1991AnApproachToTheSynthesisOfLife.pdf) · [Ofria and Wilke](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15107231/)
- **Christopher G. Langton (1984, *Physica D*) and Hiroki Sayama (1999, *Artificial Life*).** Langton's loop reproduces through local signals in a grid. Sayama's Evoloop introduces dissolution and shows selection for faster-reproducing small loops as interactions generate variants. This is directly relevant to the prediction that short words win when reproduction cost scales with length. Borrow dissolution as a condition for long-term reuse of space and compare against the turnover and size selection of a system with no linguistic mechanism. [Langton](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0167278984902562) · [Sayama](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10829086/)
- **Stephan Rafler (2011, arXiv) and Bert Wang-Chak Chan (2019, *Complex Systems*).** SmoothLife generalizes Life to continuous space and state and exhibits moving patterns. Lenia develops a related continuous CA framework and catalogs diverse localized forms and behaviors. Neither needs words to produce lifelike motion. Borrow tests for persistence, displacement, and recovery after perturbation, and report how patterns were found, including any hand-selected rules or seeds. [SmoothLife](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.1567) · [Lenia](https://www.complex-systems.com/abstracts/v28_i03_a01/)
- **Martin A. Nowak and Robert M. May (1992, *Nature*), Benjamin Kerr et al. (2002, *Nature*), and Tobias Reichenbach, Mauro Mobilia, and Erwin Frey (2007, *Nature*).** Nowak and May derive spatial patterning from local evolutionary-game payoffs. Kerr et al. show experimentally that local dispersal can maintain diversity in a cyclic bacterial competition. Reichenbach et al. show in a spatial model that mobility can organize waves and, when sufficiently high, jeopardize coexistence. Borrow a matched well-mixed control, invasion-from-rare tests, and a spatial-scale sweep. A spiral or a lasting mosaic could come from an imposed interaction cycle alone. [Nowak and May](https://www.nature.com/articles/359826a0) · [Kerr et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature00823) · [Reichenbach et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature06095)
- **Stuart A. Kauffman and Edward D. Weinberger (1989, *Journal of Theoretical Biology*) and Peter Schuster, Walter Fontana, Peter F. Stadler, and Ivo L. Hofacker (1994, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*).** NK landscapes make fitness ruggedness depend on interactions among sites. The RNA study examines how sets of sequences map to the same shape and connect through mutations. WordLife crossings can create epistasis: the merit of one letter depends on letters in two runs and on surrounding occupancy. A word ladder is neutral only under a stated phenotype or fitness equivalence. Borrow viable degree, connectedness, and accessible phenotypic variation within a neutral component. [Kauffman and Weinberger](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2632988/) · [Schuster et al.](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1994.0040)
- **Simon Kirby, Hannah Cornish, and Kenny Smith (2008, *PNAS*) and Luc Steels (1995, *Artificial Life*, volume dated 1995).** Iterated learning showed how repeated transmission through learners can yield systematic structure in an artificial-language experiment. Steels' naming games show how interacting agents can converge on shared vocabulary. This is a distinct mechanism from enforcing an inherited English lexicon. Borrow transmission bottlenecks and convergence measures if the system evolves reusable conventions. If rules fix the lexicon, investigate how its structure affects ecology rather than claiming the language itself emerged. [Kirby et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0707835105) · [Steels](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8925502/)

## 2. Blunt verdict

Crossword validity, weighted fill, WFC crossword construction, viable spelling mutations, word-triggered changes on grids, language-driven CA art, and self-reproduction with spatial competition all have precedents. A polished combination of those components could be a good demonstration. A feed of legal words appearing and vanishing is weak research evidence even if it runs indefinitely.

The plausible contribution is a causal result about a specific combination: shared letters couple live lineages, and the topology of an attested lexicon or its morphology changes the ecology under the same local rules. For example, loss of a crossing could selectively eliminate one lineage while a productive morphological family survives by switching among locally viable forms. Demonstrating that effect requires ancestry and matched controls that remove the linguistic structure while retaining comparable transition opportunities. “First word-based Game of Life,” “first emergent language,” and “open-ended evolution” are unsupported. A fixed finite lexicon on a fixed finite board can exhibit long transients, sustained stochastic turnover, and new configurations within that state space. Those observations alone say little about unbounded novelty.

## 3. The locality problem

A radius-1 update communicates at most one cell per tick. On a raw letter grid, a cell that is three steps away from an edit cannot react to it on the next tick. Two words can agree throughout the observed neighborhood and disagree in membership because of a distant letter or delimiter. A validator at an endpoint may have to wait six ticks for the opposite end of a seven-letter run, even before accounting for delimiter detection or returning a verdict. Specify which visible runs count as words, including maximal versus embedded runs, singleton handling, orientation, and board boundaries.

| Approach | What is local | What it preserves |
|---|---|---|
| Letter bigrams or trigrams | Neighboring symbols or short windows. Use boundary markers, and use centered trigrams for a radius-1 check along each axis. | English-like spelling statistics, with no general guarantee of dictionary membership. Legal windows can recombine into illegal strings. Use held-out words and generated strings to measure precision and recall. With a bounded maximum length, a sufficiently wide boundary-aware window can encode membership directly. |
| Tile adjacency or overlapping WFC | Encoded tiles agree with neighbors, or overlapping patches agree on their shared cells. Search and propagation can cover the entire board. | Letter patches preserve training patterns and can splice them into nonwords. Word-position or automaton-state tiles can enforce exact membership for every completed, properly delimited run. Standard WFC supplies a useful static baseline even when those constraints are exact. |
| Trie or DFA with states in cells | Each row cell can carry the state before or after consuming its letter, with an independent vertical track on the same letter. A boundary supplies the start state, and the far boundary requires acceptance. | Exact membership for a finite dictionary in a consistent snapshot. A prefix trie has accepting states that can also have outgoing edges. A minimized DFA can reduce state count. Edits invalidate downstream states, so live updates need a propagation protocol that distinguishes pending from accepted runs. Gilles Pesant's regular-language constraint is the finite-sequence CSP analogue. [Pesant, CP 2004](https://publications.polymtl.ca/24731/) |
| Wang tiles | In Hao Wang's edge-matching formalism, colors on abutting edges must agree. Put horizontal and vertical automaton transition labels on opposite edge pairs, require both transitions to consume the tile's displayed letter, and use delimiter tiles to enforce start and finish states. | Exact dictionary legality for well-formed finite runs in a consistent tiling. A toroidal run without a delimiter needs an explicit convention so an unconstrained state cycle cannot masquerade as a word. Checking a completed tiling is local. Finding or repairing one may require long-range changes. [Wang, *Bell System Technical Journal*, 1961](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1961.tb03975.x) |
| CA simulating sequential parsing | A signal carrying parser state traverses a run. A verdict can propagate back along it. Alvy Ray Smith III studied language recognition by local parallel arrays. | Exact recognition of a stable run with an appropriate parser. A mutable board needs a rule for edits during the scan and for simultaneous horizontal and vertical validation. Measure time spent pending and false approvals from stale state. [Smith, *Journal of Computer and System Sciences*, 1972](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000072800047) |

A span-scanning dictionary oracle is a legitimate comparison and can expose whether local validation changes the observed dynamics. For a hard length cap, a sufficiently large fixed neighborhood can implement it as a bounded-radius rule. The central tradeoff is between instantaneous letters-only validity with a larger observation radius and radius-1 dynamics with extra states, latency, or coordinated edits. Exact recognition certifies membership in the chosen list. Meaning, grammatical usage, and recognizable morphology require additional data or criteria.

## 5. Failure modes and evidence worth collecting

- **Dictionary-lookup animation.** A proposal engine that samples legal words will produce high validity by construction. A board can have high turnover simply because it is constantly reseeded. Record accepted and rejected proposals, parent–offspring events, external introductions, and the survival of descendant lineages when introductions cease. Count duplicate spellings as independent discoveries unless a transmission event connects them.
- **Static crossword or cascading collapse.** Shared cells can immobilize both words when each depends on the other, or one edit can start a chain of deletions. A global repair routine can hide the freeze by repeatedly constructing new fill. Measure activity and occupied fraction over time, absorbing-state incidence, and time to recovery after removing a small patch. Repeat with multiple seeds, sizes, boundary conditions, and update schedules.
- **Short-word or high-connectivity monocultures.** If clearance and time costs grow with length, low-cost words have a built-in advantage. ENABLE's `aa` and `ae` are candidates for this effect, especially if every embedded two-letter span earns credit. Their dominance depends on crossing opportunities and resource rules. Compare frequencies within length bands and against expected incidence from viable placements, including letter-position frequencies and local density. A minimum length can remove the symptom by construction.
- **Combinatorial explosion and an artificial clock.** A blank neighborhood can admit many word placements, while a dense one leaves almost none. Multiple crossing edits can collide. Hidden global tie-breaking, solver restarts, asynchronous ordering, and stale validation can all generate apparent waves. Report proposals and accepted edits per tick, costs by density, pending validation latency, and sensitivity to scheduling and orientation.

The decisive comparison starts with the *same* birth, death, copying, and resource rules in four conditions: the real lexicon, a synthetic lexicon matched on length and letter statistics, the real lexicon with crossing effects disabled or matched against a well-mixed interaction process, and the real lexicon with morphology links rewired within suitable length and degree classes. Add an n-gram-matched lexicon if the effect survives simple matching. For each comparison, check what the control actually preserves, particularly viable branching, mutation degree, and available placements. Otherwise generic differences in opportunity can look like linguistic effects.

A strong result would show reproducible lineage coexistence with measurable invasion from rare, spatial fronts whose direction depends on crossing compatibility, or recovery after perturbation mediated by attested morphological alternatives. Track occupancy, lineage richness and persistence, turnover, invasion growth rate, and spatial correlation length. Report effect sizes and uncertainty across independent runs after a declared burn-in, with the parameter range and failures visible. High word count, a high fraction of legal entries, a busy animation, or recognizable clusters are expected under several simpler mechanisms. The research claim earns its weight when altering an identifiable feature of the lexicon changes a measured ecological outcome and the matched controls account for generic packing and competition.
