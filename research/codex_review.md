# Adversarial review of WordLife E1 and Models A/B

E1 reproduces under the shipped controls. Model A shows no broad real-over-both-nulls advantage, but it does show reproducible structural differences between dictionaries. Model B at budget 1 penalizes real relative to Markov-2 and uniform on raw run richness. Giving the nulls the exact real short-word inventory under a shared resource composition removes almost the entire difference in run abundance and board density. Short-word membership matters in this test, and aggregate length counts and letter frequencies leave that mechanism unresolved.

Reproduction used tier 50, simulation seeds 1–5, 300 generations and snapshots every 10 generations, averaging generations 150–300 inclusive before computing sample SD across seeds. Unless shown otherwise, resource comparisons use minimum length 2 and `pDecay=0.003`. The stock generator uses null seed 12345 for each kind and minimum length.

- **Bug — advertised size and length match can silently fail.** Counts match at every length 3–15. At length 2 the sampler stops at an attempt cap, and the `Lexicon` constructor can discard generated strings ending in `s` after they have already counted toward the quota. Uniform has **fewer** two-letter words than real, and all three nulls have the same number of three-letter words as real. The larger-two-letter-count explanation for uniform is ruled out. Apply eligibility before quota counting, report achieved counts and fail explicitly on a shortfall. Enumerate eligible short support where practical. [Generator](../src/nulls.mjs) · [Lexicon filter](../src/lexicon.mjs)
- **Confound — matching the generator is weaker than matching the realized dictionary.** Markov-2 suppresses the stop transition before the target length and checks only that termination is possible at the final context. This is a different distribution from the original chain conditioned on length, which weights paths by continuation and termination probabilities. Uniqueness and disjoint rejection further reshape the sample. At minimum length 2 the result shifts `i` by +0.485 and `n` by −0.472 percentage points. Uniform proposes i.i.d. letters weighted by real type frequencies, so its name describes independence rather than an equiprobable alphabet. Test a fixed-length conditional chain, publish realized marginal distributions by length and position, and use a common proposal prior or resource mix for model comparisons. [Generator](../src/nulls.mjs) · [Crystal weighting](../src/models/crystal.mjs)
- **OK with qualification — attestation support.** Markov-2 keeps a subset of the real internal bigrams. Both minimum lengths have the exact same sets of 26 start letters and 24 end letters as real. Uniform and shuffle add internal pairs and allow all 26 end letters. These are embedded bigrams, distinct from valid standalone two-letter words. For a radius-one attestation control, use identical binary tables explicitly. For a frequency-matched claim, also measure realized bigram and trigram frequencies. [Generator](../src/nulls.mjs) · [Attestation tables](../src/lexicon.mjs)
- **Confound — excluding real words.** Disjointness is suitable for asking what synthetic-only dictionaries do. For a statistical null conditioned on the trained spelling model, it deliberately removes real-like outcomes and can distort short-string compatibility, assembly and edit edges. In a one-seed sensitivity check at minimum length 2, dropping only the disjointness veto admitted 1,212 real spellings into Markov-2 (20 dimers, 169 trimers, 1,023 longer). Its substitution degree rose from 0.682 to 0.751 and reachability from 30.68% to 34.68%. Uniform admitted 115 spellings, with degree changing from 0.175 to 0.177 and reachability from 6.56% to 6.64%. E1's directions survive this check. Report overlap, include overlap-allowed controls and sample several null dictionaries to measure construction variance. Broader claims about English should treat the source dictionary and tier as part of the design. [Generator](../src/nulls.mjs) · [Fixed null cache and simulation seeds](../experiments/run.mjs)

## E1 and Model A

- **OK — E1 reproduced.** At minimum length 2, real versus Markov-2 gives substitution degree 1.57 versus 0.68, growth reachability 28.3% versus 30.7% and length-seven bigram precision \(3.98 × 10^{-6}\) versus \(4.20 × 10^{-6}\). At minimum length 3, reachability is 21.1% versus 23.6%. The wildcard groups count each substitution edge from each endpoint, and the reachability recurrence correctly reconstructs one-dimensional birth sequences from two strictly shorter pieces. Reachability is a support calculation with idealized starting letters and no perpendicular constraints, time costs or resource costs. A trimer can form at minimum length 3 by bridging two singletons. The precision calculation gives each locally admissible string equal weight. It cannot estimate board yield under the model's weighted, correlated dynamics. [E1 implementation](../experiments/lexstats.mjs)
- **Confound — Model A is close in gross density, with measurable structural differences.** At minimum length 2, real, Markov-2 and uniform densities were 0.622 ± 0.005, 0.631 ± 0.003 and 0.610 ± 0.004, respectively. Species counts were 327 ± 5, 386 ± 6 and 315 ± 8. Relative ordering depends on the null and metric. Mean real run length was 2.685, so “short-word lattice” is more accurate than “two-letter lattice.” At minimum length 3, real density was 0.080 ± 0.010 versus 0.091 ± 0.008 and 0.087 ± 0.009, with valid run abundance 13.3 ± 6.4 versus 22.3 ± 6.0 and 21.8 ± 5.8. The sparse regime reproduces, while the direction and magnitude of its differences are metric-dependent. [Crystal rules](../src/models/crystal.mjs) · [Metrics](../src/metrics.mjs)
- **Confound — the viable mutation advantage is small relative to seed spread.** Real averaged 0.362 ± 0.162 neutral mutations per sampled generation, versus 0.325 ± 0.112 for Markov-2 and 0.287 ± 0.095 for uniform. That is about +26% versus uniform and +12% versus Markov-2 under these settings. Viable mode chooses an alternative only if both runs allow it, so these counts mix exposure to mutation attempts with availability of at least one local alternative. Instrument attempts, count weighted legal alternatives, and normalize accepted mutations per attempt. [Mutation branch](../src/models/crystal.mjs)
- **Confound — turnover snapshots do not establish freezing.** The roughly 0.4% result is deaths in the last generation divided by occupancy after that generation, at every tenth generation. Event counters reset every step. Seed-level averaging before computing SD is appropriate. Sparse snapshots are noisy for rare mutations, and death-only turnover misses changes in spelling and topology. Aggregate per-step deaths over the occupied-cell exposure and add lagged cell and run persistence before describing a frozen phase. [Batch summarizer](../experiments/compare.mjs) · [Runner](../experiments/run.mjs)

## Model B and short-word controls

Stock budget 1 gave species 2.79 ± 0.46 real, 5.01 ± 0.54 Markov-2, 6.68 ± 1.55 uniform and 1.60 ± 0.36 shuffle. This reproduces the direction versus uniform, with different means from 3.2 versus 8.0 and an opposite direction versus shuffle. Stock budget 3 yielded 254 ± 9, 326 ± 4 and 309 ± 5 for real, Markov-2 and uniform. The real-versus-uniform difference persists when the budget is raised, while the budget-1 regime has much lower abundance.

I reran both models at minimum length 2 using the real lexicon's letter frequencies for proposals in A and for starting resource composition in B. For the “real short” conditions, I replaced every null spelling of length 2 or 3 with the real spellings at those lengths, retaining the null entries of length 4 and up. The real rows are the common reference. The swap changes string identities and also restores any short-count deficit. Uniform initially has a *smaller* inventory, which separates its original advantage from the proposed larger-inventory explanation.

- **Confound — resource composition and dictionary both change in stock Model B.** At budget 1, the 13-cell interior catchment needs a starting frequency of at least 1/13 ≈ 7.69% for a letter to be initially drawable. Near the threshold, a small shift can change which singles and early runs can form. Holding composition fixed reduces the Markov-2 abundance advantage and leaves a large uniform advantage; sharing real short strings then removes both abundance gaps. Uniform retains a residual richness advantage at similar run abundance, so the swap is insufficient to explain every diversity difference. Test separate dimer/trimer swaps, equal-count resamples, and shared per-letter starting resources. [Resource initialization and draw](../src/models/crystal.mjs)
- **OK with qualification — species measures active-dictionary spelling richness.** The same counting rule applies to all runs, and the shared-composition control shows that richness can differ at similar abundance. Raw richness still rises with the number of observed runs and the availability and relative abundance of eligible spellings. At budget 1 the stock uniform boards hold about 108 valid runs versus 49 for real. Rarefy to equal numbers of valid runs within each length and report run abundance and evenness separately. Since runs lack ancestry, species here is a label for unique spellings. [Species measure](../src/metrics.mjs)
- **Bug — `starved` combines resource limitation with spelling failure.** An attempted birth with zero dictionary-valid candidates increments the same counter as one with valid candidates that are all unaffordable. Failed isolated rain due to supply does not increment it. Count no legal spelling and no affordable legal spelling separately and label birth versus rain. The reproduced densities and diversity counts do not use this counter. [Birth branch](../src/models/crystal.mjs)
- **Bug — resource conservation can fail when seeding.** `plant()` writes directly through `Grid.plant()`, bypassing consumption and recycling when it writes or overwrites tiles. With `resources=true`, positive `seedWords` adds free board letters to the initial budget. The reproduced runs use zero seed words. Make resource planting transactional, or account for planted letters in the initial composition before diffusion. Also validate the diffusion coefficient against the explicit stencil's nonnegative range (0–0.25 for degree-four interiors). The default 0.2 is in range. [Crystal seeding and diffusion](../src/models/crystal.mjs) · [Grid planting](../src/grid.mjs)

The results support a narrower claim than (d): under these crossword rules, short valid-string inventories strongly shape the measured board regimes. Matching the length histogram and approximate letter marginals does not make inventories interchangeable. Stock comparisons also conflate the realized dictionary with the proposal prior or resource composition. To test a residual English-specific effect, share these factors, compare multiple null draws, and report abundance and length-standardized richness together.
